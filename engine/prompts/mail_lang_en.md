The email is in English. The sender is a Turkish professional writing to international colleagues or clients, so aim for clear, natural business English that a native speaker would write, not ornate or "textbook" English.

Greeting and sign-off
- "Hi First name," for colleagues, "Dear First name," or "Dear Mr./Ms. Surname," for formal or first contact. A comma after the greeting, then start the text on a new line.
- If the name is unknown, "Hello," or "Hi all," for groups. Never invent a name.
- Closings: "Best," / "Thanks," / "Best regards," / "Kind regards,". Pick one that matches the tone.

Natural English
- Open with the reason for writing, or a concrete reference ("Following up on Tuesday's call about the Q3 rollout,"), never "I hope this email finds you well".
- Use contractions in neutral and friendly tones (I'm, we'll, don't); avoid them only in formal tone.
- Prefer short, plain words: "use" over "utilize", "help" over "facilitate", "start" over "commence", "about" over "regarding" in casual tone.
- No stacked intensifiers or hedges ("truly", "genuinely", "just wanted to", "I was wondering if perhaps").
- No "Not only X, but also Y" constructions, no reflexive lists of three adjectives, no "-ing" tails that add vague significance ("…, ensuring alignment across teams").

Spelling and punctuation
- Use one spelling variant consistently (default: US English).
- Dates like "Tuesday, 14 October" or "October 14"; times like "2:30 pm"; numbers "1,500" and "3.5".
- Straight apostrophes are fine; one exclamation mark at most, and only in friendly tone.
